Lesson 01 · About 3 minutes · Understand your portfolio

A photograph,
a drawing,
a breeze.

The forest scene looks like one image. It is several layers, and each has a different job.

Your win: know which layer to change when something looks wrong.

01 / Atmosphere

JPEG photograph

assets/images/forest.jpg supplies the faint forest behind the content. A JPEG stores a grid of colored pixels.

02 / Shape

SVG drawing

The foreground tree in index.html is drawn from paths. Its leaves are reusable shapes with individual positions.

03 / Movement

Anime.js breeze

script.js slowly rotates groups of SVG shapes. The photograph’s individual leaves are not being animated.

Sources: MDN: raster and vector graphics · Anime.js: animation · this portfolio’s source files.

You spotted a real gap.

I placed some leaf bases slightly away from the curved stem. That twig was reused throughout the tree, so its small placement errors repeated. Checking the drawing with motion disabled confirmed the gaps. The portfolio now has corrected joins; this diagram preserves the earlier version so you can compare.

Moving a drawing does not repair its joins. Think of paper leaves glued to a paper twig: waving the whole piece preserves any gaps that were already there.

Explore the layers

The original leaf positions are shown.

Your reduced-motion preference keeps the breeze off.

Try this: hide the photo, turn on the breeze, then align the bases. Notice that changing the photo cannot close an SVG gap.

A good joint needs both correct placement and a fixed pivot. Here, aligning the leaf bases fixes this twig. Larger leaf clusters also need their bases positioned on their branches.

Why the twig moves together: MDN: SVG groups. Why the pin stays fixed: MDN: rotation around a chosen point.
